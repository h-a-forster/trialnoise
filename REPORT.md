# trialnoise report: variance components of the Terminal-Bench 2 leaderboard

Generated by `trialnoise report` from [results/v1](results/v1); edit
`src/trialnoise/report_text.py`, not this file. Model, estimators and the changes to the original
plan are in [docs/DESIGN.md](docs/DESIGN.md). Intervals are 95%. A point is one percentage point of
pass rate.

## Summary

- **How many trials and tasks does a 3-point gap need?** It depends on the question. To say one
  agent beat another on these 89 tasks: 17 trials per task. To say it will beat the
  other on tasks like these, no number of trials on 89 tasks is enough: 717 tasks at 5
  trials for two scaffolds on one model, 1008 when both differ. A 10-point gap on tasks
  like these is within reach of the current benchmark: 3 to 6 trials on 89 tasks.
- **More tasks beat more trials.** At today's 89 x 5, a near pair with a pass rate of at least 60%
  has an MDE of 8.6 to 10.2 points. Doubling the trials gives 7.7 to
  9.4. Going to 150 tasks at 5 trials gives 6.6 to 7.8 for
  fewer trials. Trial noise is 40% of a same-model pair's task-level variance at 5
  trials; the rest is task x cell, which repeats cannot touch, so doubling trials removes only half
  of that share.
- **Variance of one trial:** task 31.2%, cell 14.6%, task x cell 20.3%,
  trial 33.9%, on 108 cells x 89 tasks and 46,830 trials. ICC(1) is 0.60
  (0.551 to 0.648) and ICC(1,5) is 0.88 (0.860 to 0.902).
- **Scaffold against model depends on where you look.** Across the 18 models seen under
  at least three scaffolds, scaffold-within-model is 5.9% of the variance of cell scores
  (3.4 to 15.3): the model decides most of the score. Among the 6 models whose cells
  average at least 50% it is 37.2% (23.2 to 100.0), but that figure is set by the cut: 18.2%
  at 40% and 47.6% at 60%. Selecting models on their score removes model variance. The
  scaffold SD holds at 4.3 to 5.1 points at every cut, and the
  model SD falls from 10.3 to 4.5. Scaffolds reshape which tasks get
  solved less than models do, though the data cannot rule out parity: within pass-rate bands, task x
  scaffold is 0.0097 (0.003 to 0.016) and task x model 0.0159 (0.010 to 0.022).
- **Run-to-run shifts are small here, and now measured.** 20 submissions with replicate jobs
  give a pooled run variance on 72 df that estimates below zero, with an upper limit of
  0.9 points of run SD, against evalnoise's 2.9 points (1.6 to 6.4) on 7 df. Allowing for
  that limit moves a same-model MDE from 8.6 to 9.3 points. These are jobs a
  submitter chose to publish, so an unselected rerun could vary more.
- **The calculator holds out of sample.** On 252 near pairs among 39 submissions
  the trajectory dataset never saw, observed paired SEs were a median 0.94 of the planner's
  prediction, and 83% were within 20% of it. Separately, simulation from the fit checks
  the planner's test, not new data: size 0.046 to 0.051, and power at the planned
  MDE 0.81 to 0.83 for a gain and 0.77 to 0.78 for a loss.
- **The LessWrong figure holds, and adjacent pairs are the wrong test.** With a task-clustered
  paired test, 0 of 25 adjacent best-per-scaffold pairs separate at Holm 0.05
  (0 at BH, 2 unadjusted), against the post's 1 of 25. That set keeps the copied
  cell, to match the post's 26 rows. Over all 107 adjacent cells it is 0, with a
  median gap of 0.45 points. Doubling trials would change nothing: a simulated board separates
  0.4% of best-per-scaffold pairs at 5 trials and 0.4% at 10, near the 0.2%
  that one rejected pair on one board in twenty gives. Whole-board pairs do separate:
  56% of 5,778 at Holm, none closer than 10.6 points.
- **Lost verdicts are rare but uneven.** 1.4% of trials in 60 complete official
  submissions ended with no verdict, which the leaderboard counts as a failure. That is 0.44%
  of the variance of their scores and costs a median 0.42 points, but up to 9.9 for
  one submission. Agent timeouts, scored as usual, hit 14.3% of trials.
- **Replicate noise is an integrity signal (exploratory).** `MAYA__Claude-4.5-sonnet` has five jobs with identical
  outcomes on all 89 tasks, so it is left out. Four of the six submissions from the three agents the
  benchmark's organisers named for cheating or reward hacking rank in the lowest 9 of
  148 for how often trials of one task disagree.

## Data

Two pinned sources, reduced to two committed tables by `trialnoise extract`.

The trajectory dataset (`yoonholee/terminalbench-trajectories`) lists 52,104 rows. 4,952 are the
same trial listed twice, one row from each of two scrapes (their rewards agree), which leaves
**47,152 trials** in 109 cells on 89 tasks. It has no exception field, and its
`cost_cents` is 0, not null, where nothing was reported.

The official leaderboard repository (`harborframework/terminal-bench-2-leaderboard`) is 121 GB, but
each job's `result.json` names every trial by reward and by exception: 31,890 trials,
30,993 with a verdict, in about 3 MB. 10,772 trials in 27 cells
link to the trajectory table by trial name, and every linked reward agrees. Those files also give
what the dataset lacks: job membership and exceptions.

Task x cell combinations by trial count: 269 with 1, 9 with 2, 13 with 3, 195 with 4, 9,032 with 5, 94 with 6. The score here is the task-equal mean. The
leaderboard's own mean is passes over designed trials, which counts a missing verdict as a failure.

## Variance components

Moment estimates, cross-checked by REML on the 108 x 89 cell means (task 0.0764, cell
0.0356, task x cell 0.0497). Degrees of freedom: task 88, cell 107, task x
cell 9,416, trial 37,218.

| Term | Variance | SD, points | Share | 95% CI, tasks and cells | 95% CI, tasks only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Task | 0.0764 | 27.6 | 31.2% | 0.060 to 0.094 | 0.061 to 0.091 |
| Cell | 0.0356 | 18.9 | 14.6% | 0.026 to 0.047 | 0.029 to 0.045 |
| Task x cell | 0.0497 | 22.3 | 20.3% | 0.043 to 0.054 | 0.044 to 0.054 |
| Trial | 0.0830 | 28.8 | 33.9% | 0.073 to 0.093 | 0.074 to 0.092 |

Use the first interval. The tasks-only bootstrap the plan called for holds cells fixed, and in
simulation it covers the cell term only 0.73 of the time.

ICC(1), the correlation of two trials of one cell on one task with the cell held fixed, is
0.603. With cells treated as random the same correlation is 0.66. Per cell it has a
median of 0.61, a 10th to 90th percentile range of 0.44 to 0.69 and a minimum of
0.09, close to the 0.5 to 0.8 in the LessWrong post. That reliability is high because tasks
differ, and a paired comparison removes the task main effect: with it gone the same correlation is
0.37 (0.330 to 0.406).

Sensitivity (`components.csv`): keeping the copied cell, dropping the top cell, dropping cells
without repeats, or dropping linked trials with no verdict moves task x cell between 0.0491 and
0.0516, and ICC(1) between 0.601 and 0.606.

### Pooled task x cell overstates what near pairs face

The additive model's task x cell, 0.0497, averages over pairs like gpt-oss-20b against Opus
4.6, whose task profiles barely overlap. Pairs within 10 points of each other imply far less, and
how much less depends on what they share:

| Near pairs | Pairs | Task x cell | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| same-model | 149 | 0.0232 | 0.016 to 0.030 |
| same-scaffold | 251 | 0.0269 | 0.020 to 0.034 |
| neither | 1190 | 0.0386 | 0.031 to 0.046 |

Read as sums, the three split task x cell by source. The comparison types sit at different pass
rates, and task x cell moves with the pass rate, so the split is taken inside each band:

| Pass-rate band | Task x model | Task x scaffold | Task x model x scaffold |
|---|---|---|---|
| low | 0.0144 (0.007 to 0.022) | 0.0076 (0.002 to 0.014) | 0.0095 (0.001 to 0.018) |
| mid | 0.0169 (0.007 to 0.027) | 0.0136 (0.005 to 0.022) | 0.0130 (0.002 to 0.024) |
| high | 0.0163 (0.006 to 0.028) | 0.0079 (-0.008 to 0.023) | 0.0166 (-0.000 to 0.035) |
| Mean of the three | 0.0159 (0.010 to 0.022) | 0.0097 (0.003 to 0.016) | 0.0130 (0.005 to 0.021) |

Averaged over the bands, task x model exceeds task x scaffold by 0.0062 (-0.002 to 0.015): scaffolds
look like the smaller source, but the interval does not exclude parity. So the planner uses strata.
A plan from the pooled term would say 10.9 points where a same-model pair in the high band
has 8.6.

## Run term

evalnoise's weakest number was a run variance on 7 df. Here a job is a run, and 20
submissions hold replicate jobs on at least 80 tasks. Their pooled Henderson III estimate is
-0.00017 on 72 df, and the Burdick-Graybill upper limit is 0.000081, a run SD of
0.9 points. So the planner uses zero, with that limit as a sensitivity. This is 72
df, not the hundreds the plan hoped for: the other cells ran as one job, which confounds their run
effect with the cell.

The job means agree slightly better than chance would (F = 0.78, lower-tail p = 0.08).
Submitters may have kept jobs that agree. An A/A check fits the estimate: splitting each five-job
submission 2 against 3, a paired test rejects 5 of 160 splits (3.1%,
nominal 5%; splits share jobs, so they are not independent).

## Infrastructure errors

Exceptions exist only in official job files, so this covers official submissions. Over 60
complete submissions in good standing (27,145 trials), 1.4% of trials had no verdict
(median 0.9%, worst 14.4%). Counted as failures, as the leaderboard does, they cost a
median 0.42 points and at most 9.9. Their SD across submissions is 1.35
points, 0.44% of the variance of scores, and the rank correlation between the two scores is
0.994. Anthropic's post measured infrastructure errors of 5.8%, falling to 2.1% with looser
resource limits. The public job files show less, but a job file records only a trial's last attempt,
and only the jobs a submitter kept, so these are lower bounds.

`AgentTimeoutError` covers 14.3% of those trials, and most keep a verdict
(3,767 of 3,887; 573 passed). It is a task limit, not an error, but a resource-starved
environment would raise it too, which this data cannot separate. By submission: `infra.csv`.

## Integrity

This section was not planned. It came from reading the replicate jobs, and the screen below was
built after seeing the outcome, so treat it as a lead, not a test.

`MAYA__Claude-4.5-sonnet` (`final/claude-sonnet-4-5` in the trajectory table) has five jobs whose trial outcomes are
identical on all 89 tasks. The typical cell has both a pass and a failure on about a third of tasks,
so five independent runs do not do this. Those jobs hold one run's information, and the cell is left
out of the primary fit. Whether it was a copy or a replay, the result files alone cannot say.

The share of tasks with both a pass and a failure, against pass rate (robust quadratic fit, z-scores
in `integrity.csv`), flags 2 of 148 submissions at z below -3: `MAYA__Claude-4.5-sonnet`, `hookele__gpt5.1-codex-mini`. The second
has 70 jobs split by task and no known problem. The organisers' integrity post names three agents
(OB-1, Pilot, ForgeCode), which here means six submissions. Their ranks from the bottom are
3, 4, 6, 9, 27, 33; the chance that one of them sits below any other submission is 0.93. Solutions that reach the agent make outcomes repeat, which fits. But the
screen flags by itself only the most extreme case, and low noise is also what a strong, stable agent
shows.

`forge/gemini-3.1-pro`, the top cell at 78.4%, is one of those named (rank 6).
The organisers say its affected trials were rescored to 0. Both sources agree trial for trial at
the pinned commits and it still tops the board, so neither appears to include that rescoring.
Dropping it changes the components little (`components.csv`).

## Limitations

- Cells are submissions, not a sample. The tasks-and-cells interval treats them as exchangeable,
  which they are not: submitters choose what to run and what to publish, and may tune to these
  tasks.
- Components are on the probability scale, where variance depends on the pass rate. The strata
  handle this for planning. Shares do not carry over to a benchmark with a different pass rate.
- "150 tasks" assumes new tasks like these 89. If tasks are added to catch what these miss, task x
  cell could grow.
- Task-level inference assumes independent tasks. If tasks share themes, the MDEs are too small.
- The run term rests on published jobs in 20 mostly strong submissions, whose first and last
  jobs are a median 2 days apart. Provider or version drift over months is a different
  quantity. The crossed-model residual,
  SD 3.4 points, is the only bound on it here.
- The frontier rows select models on their score, so the scaffold share there depends on the cut.
- Adjacent ranks are chosen from the same data they are tested on, which inflates gaps. That does
  not change the conclusion that they do not separate, but the fixed-task Holm count for
  best-per-scaffold pairs (3) is exposed to it.
- Exception data cover official submissions only, so none of the earliest large runs. Retried
  attempts leave no trace.

## What would change the conclusion

- **A real run term.** The planner sets run variance to zero, from jobs that 20 submitters
  chose to publish. Reruns chosen by someone else with a run SD near evalnoise's 2.9 points, not the
  0.9 upper limit here, would widen every MDE and leave a run-blind paired test wrong
  0.227 of the time. Drift over months, bounded here only by the crossed residual of SD
  3.4 points, would do the same.
- **New tasks unlike these 89.** "More tasks beat more trials" holds if added tasks resemble these
  and are independent. If new tasks raise task x cell, or tasks cluster by theme, the tasks needed
  grow and the MDEs here are too small.
- **The calculator off the high band.** 232 of 252 held-out pairs are in the
  high band. Low- or mid-band pairs whose observed SEs stray from the prediction, where the median
  here is 0.94, would mean those rows of the calculator are wrong.
- **Scaffold against model.** The scaffold SD of 4.3 to 5.1
  points comes from submissions run on different dates and routes. A design that crosses scaffolds
  and models on one date could move it. The frontier share moves with the cut by construction.
- **Adjacent pairs.** That neighbours do not separate holds for every simulated design, up to 300
  tasks (7.9% of best-per-scaffold pairs). It would change only if cells spread further
  apart than the 10.6-point smallest gap separated at Holm.
- **Integrity.** The screen was built after seeing the outcome. A test on a benchmark it was not
  built on could confirm it or drop it.

## Reproduce

```sh
uv sync --group dev
uv run trialnoise fetch       # 224 MB, hashes checked
uv run trialnoise extract     # rebuild the two trial tables
uv run trialnoise study       # about 5 minutes
uv run trialnoise validate    # about 2 minutes
uv run trialnoise report      # this file
uv run trialnoise check       # every output byte-identical
```
